# Hegel formalization guardrails

> **Status:** research constraints, not a positive formalization. This note records discrepancies exposed by comparing the repository's current Bowman and Rocq notes with selected essays in Jon Stewart's *The Hegel Myths and Legends*. The anthology contains competing interpretations and some polemical historical claims; it should be used here to identify mistakes a formal model must avoid, not as a single authoritative reading of Hegel.

## Why this note is needed

The repository already gets several important things right:

- `README.md` says that the finite categorical model is a deliberately small proposed model, not a formalization of Hegel as a whole.
- `bowman-absolute-negativity.md` distinguishes ordinary logical negation, determinate negation, and absolute negativity, and warns against turning absolute negativity into a truth table or an unexamined primitive first principle.
- `rocq-as-trusted-metatheory.md` separates uncertain observations, explicit assumptions, executable inference, proof of soundness, and philosophical interpretation.

The comparison nevertheless exposes several missing guardrails. They matter before choosing any stronger formal vocabulary because otherwise the implementation can accidentally encode familiar caricatures of Hegel and then verify those caricatures perfectly.

## 1. Do not encode thesis-antithesis-synthesis as Hegel's general method

Gustav E. Mueller's essay "The Hegel Legend of 'Thesis-Antithesis-Synthesis'" attacks the familiar picture in which every Hegelian argument is generated by a fixed three-step schema. The important point for this repository is independent of Mueller's more polemical historical genealogy: a three-part formal object is not thereby a representation of "the Hegelian dialectic."

Mueller points out that Hegel himself criticizes a mechanically prescribed triplicity as a lifeless or spiritless schema. He also emphasizes that Hegel's texts do not proceed by a globally applied thesis-antithesis-synthesis template. Jon Stewart's introduction likewise treats the slogan as one of the standard Hegel legends.

**Constraint for the formalization:**

- Do not introduce constructors named `Thesis`, `Antithesis`, and `Synthesis` as the default shape of dialectical movement.
- Do not infer philosophical significance merely because a candidate category, transition system, or proof has three objects, three stages, or a threefold decomposition.
- Any threefold structure must be justified from the particular Hegelian passage or interpretation being modeled.

This is especially important for the finite Lawvere experiment in `README.md`: a small categorical structure can be a useful test object without being promoted into a universal dialectical schema.

## 2. "Contradiction" is not simply `P ∧ ¬P`

The Bowman note already says that absolute negativity is not ordinary Boolean negation and that the point is not simply "everything is contradiction." Robert Pippin's "Hegel's Metaphysics and the Problem of Contradiction" makes the required qualification more exact.

Pippin locates the problem in Hegel's attempt to state what something *is*—its determinacy or essence—while accounting for its relations to appearances and differences. On Pippin's reconstruction, Hegel is not claiming that every sentence is both true and false or that ordinary discourse is straightforwardly contradictory. The problem arises in the metaphysical account of determination, especially the identity-and-difference of essence and appearance.

Pippin's discussion also makes clear why the common example `s is P` / `s is not P` is dangerous if read as a propositional contradiction. The second "is not" can concern non-identity between a particular and a universal rather than the simple denial of a predicate. Hegel's difficult claim is about whether an adequate determination of identity must include difference, not about licensing arbitrary inconsistent propositions.

**Constraint for the formalization:**

- Never translate Hegelian `contradiction` directly to a theorem of the form `P /\ ~P` merely because the source uses the word *Widerspruch*.
- Type or otherwise distinguish ordinary propositional negation from relations of identity, difference, dependence, determination, and self-relation.
- A candidate model should state exactly what its formal analogue of "contradiction" is before proving anything about it.
- A proof that an object-level Hegelian relation has some property must not be presented as a proof that classical noncontradiction fails.

This sharpens, rather than reverses, the existing Bowman note.

## 3. Hegel's critique of common logic does not require making Rocq nonclassical

Robert Hanna's "From an Ontological Point of View: Hegel's Critique of the Common Logic" is directly relevant to `rocq-as-trusted-metatheory.md`. Hanna argues that Hegel's speculative logic is not a bare denial or replacement of common logic. Common logic retains its integrity and correctness at its own level; Hegel's criticism concerns its claim to ontological adequacy when its forms are treated as exhaustive descriptions of reality.

Hanna applies this point to judgment, syllogism, identity, and contradiction. In his reading, Hegel's higher-order criticisms are ontological criticisms, not proposals that ordinary logical inference should cease to work. He explicitly rejects the interpretation on which Hegel simply denies the principle of noncontradiction.

**Constraint for the formalization:**

- Keep Rocq's trusted metatheory ordinary and explicit.
- Do not modify the proof theory merely to make it look "dialectical."
- Put speculative/Hegelian claims in the object language or in separately named structures, where their assumptions can be inspected.
- `infer_sound` should mean soundness with respect to the rules actually declared, not a special Hegelian notion of validity.

This gives a stronger reason for the architecture already proposed in `rocq-as-trusted-metatheory.md`: Rocq can check the bookkeeping of inference without pretending that Rocq's kernel *is* Hegel's speculative logic.

## 4. The target is not formalization itself, but an unwarranted claim of ontological exhaustiveness

Hanna also helps avoid the opposite mistake. Hegel's criticism of mechanical or calculative logical forms is not, on Hanna's account, a simple objection to mathematics, formal languages, or formalization. The objection is to treating a form that works at one level as though it exhaustively captured organic, dynamic, or concrete relations at another.

**Constraint for the formalization:**

A finite category, relation algebra, transition system, or Rocq development is legitimate as a test instrument if its scope is explicit. The repository should reject the inference

```text
formally verified model
therefore
Hegel's ontology has this structure
```

unless an independent interpretive argument supplies the second step.

This is consistent with the current README's modest scope and should remain an invariant as the formal development grows.

## 5. Bowman's absolute negativity should remain a strong candidate interpretation, not an axiom disguised as a result

The current Bowman note is substantially aligned with Bowman's own framing and with Dean Moyar's review: the Concept is treated as a relational structure and absolute negativity as its generative, self-relating dynamic; determinate negation is downstream of that more basic self-relation.

The important unresolved issue is already mentioned in the existing note but should constrain implementation. Moyar objects that an autonomous absolute negativity can begin to look like the sort of first principle Hegel is supposed to reject. He also questions whether Bowman's skepticism about finite cognition is too strong and whether the *Doctrine of Essence* is carrying explanatory weight that the later *Doctrine of the Concept* should carry.

**Constraint for the formalization:**

- Do not declare `AbsoluteNegativity` as an unexplained primitive generator and then count derivations from it as evidence for Bowman.
- If a candidate structure generates difference from self-relation, record which properties are assumptions and which are derived.
- Test whether the construction has quietly presupposed the relata or the difference it claims to generate.
- Keep Bowman's reading labeled as an interpretation under test, not as a synonym for "Hegel."

## 6. Future historical or social models must distinguish "actual" from merely existent

Jon Stewart's "Hegel and the Myth of Reason" rejects the familiar inference from Hegel's claim about the rational and the actual to the proposition that every existing institution or historical event is rational and justified. Stewart emphasizes Hegel's own allowance that existing laws or institutions can be wrong and irrational.

This issue is not required for the present finite identity/opposition experiment, but it is a useful repository-level guardrail if the project later formalizes historical, political, or social examples.

Do **not** encode a rule such as

```text
Exists x -> Rational x
```

or

```text
Exists x -> Justified x
```

as though it followed from Hegel's use of *Wirklichkeit*.

## Practical invariants for the next formal branch

Until a particular formal model is chosen, the following invariants are enough:

1. **No universal triad.** No global thesis-antithesis-synthesis machinery.
2. **No equivocation on negation.** Propositional negation, determinate difference, and absolute negativity remain distinct.
3. **No automatic inconsistency.** Hegelian contradiction is not silently translated to `P /\ ~P`.
4. **Classical trusted layer stays trusted.** Rocq verifies explicit rules; it is not rewritten to simulate speculative logic.
5. **Object language carries interpretation.** Philosophical hypotheses live in named structures with inspectable assumptions.
6. **No formalism-to-ontology jump.** Machine verification proves facts about the formal object, not automatically facts about Hegel.
7. **No primitive victory.** If a structure is supposed to generate difference, tests must check that difference was not assumed in advance.
8. **Source status stays visible.** Distinguish Hegel's text, an interpreter's reconstruction, and this repository's formal analogue.

### Source-handling warning

The Stewart anthology is useful precisely because it gathers arguments against standard caricatures, but its contributors do not constitute a single interpretive school. Mueller's essay in particular mixes a valuable anti-schema argument with polemical claims about Marx and Marxism. The repository should preserve the former as a guardrail without adopting the latter as established historical fact. Where a genealogy matters to the formal project, it should be checked independently against primary sources and later scholarship.
